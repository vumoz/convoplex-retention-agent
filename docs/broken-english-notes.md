# Broken English notes

Notes from Victor, a native speaker. This is for people reading the agent in English. Messages to customers call the language Broken English, not Pidgin.

## What it is

Broken English (Nigerian Pidgin) draws on English words. Speakers shorten them or respell them by sound. It also has its own vocabulary, and that vocabulary is still growing. It is not a word-for-word copy of English, and it is not a caricature.

A voice transcriber that expects English often writes the sound `wan` as "won't". In Broken English, `wan` means want. "I wan buy" means "I want to buy".

## A short glossary

These are common and safe to take at the usual meaning. The list is not complete.

| Broken English | English |
| --- | --- |
| wan | want. "I wan buy" = "I want to buy". |
| abeg | please |
| wahala | trouble |
| sabi | know |
| chop | eat |
| how much e be | what's the price |
| kpai | a softer word for die. "e don kpai" = he, she, or it is dead. |

## Bereavement

If a customer mentions a death or a bereavement, including `kpai` or "don die", the reply is sympathy only. Do not push the offer, a price, or a discount in that same message. Wait for the customer to come back to the shop.

## In the agent

The reply system prompt carries this same short glossary. The customer-facing name stays Broken English. Igbo, Yoruba, and Hausa wording elsewhere is still pending native-speaker review. These Broken English notes are from a native speaker.
